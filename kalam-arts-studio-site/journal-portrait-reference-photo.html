How to choose a reference photo for a custom portrait

A clear reference photograph gives a portrait artist more information to work with. Use this checklist when choosing the image for a custom pencil drawing.

Choose a sharp image

The eyes, face and key features should be in focus. Avoid screenshots, compressed images and photos that become pixelated when zoomed.

Look for useful lighting

Soft, even light usually makes facial features easier to read. Very strong shadows can hide details, although a dramatic reference may be appropriate when requested.

Keep the face visible

Choose a photo where the face is not blocked by hands, sunglasses, hair or other objects unless those details are an intentional part of the portrait.

Send the original file

Whenever possible, share the original photo rather than a cropped or edited social-media copy. If you have two or three options, send them together.

Explain what matters

Tell the studio about any important details, preferred crop, number of people, background preference and whether the portrait is intended as a gift or keepsake.

Think about the final composition

The best reference is not always the most attractive social-media photograph. Choose an image that gives enough information for the crop you want. A close-up may be useful for a head-and-shoulders drawing, while a wider image may be needed when clothing or body posture is important.

When several people are included

If the portrait combines family members from different photographs, send the original images separately and explain the intended arrangement. The studio will need to review whether the lighting, angle and scale can be brought together convincingly.

What to send with the photograph

Include the preferred size, number of people, crop, any important details, whether the portrait is a gift and the date it is needed. These details help the studio assess the work before discussing a quote.
