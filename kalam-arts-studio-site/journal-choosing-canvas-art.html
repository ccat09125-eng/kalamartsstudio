How to choose custom canvas art for your space

The best custom artwork begins with the space, the mood and the scale. Consider these points before commissioning a canvas.

Measure the wall

Note the available wall width and height, furniture below it and viewing distance. A small canvas can disappear on a large wall; an oversized work can overwhelm a compact space.

Decide the mood

Think about whether the room should feel calm, expressive, warm, dramatic or minimal. Share reference images that communicate the mood, not only a specific painting style.

Build a considered palette

Look at the main colours in the room, including flooring, furniture and textiles. The artwork can complement the palette or introduce a deliberate contrast.

Choose orientation and composition

Landscape, portrait and square formats create different visual rhythms. The subject and the available wall space should guide the composition.

Share practical details

Send approximate dimensions, preferred medium, intended location and any delivery or installation considerations when requesting a custom artwork.

Think about viewing distance and lighting

A canvas is usually viewed from a particular position in a room. Consider where people sit or walk, how much natural or artificial light reaches the wall and whether reflections may affect the finished surface. Share a photograph taken from the normal viewing position.

Use a reference as direction, not an assumption of a copy

Reference images are useful for discussing subject, palette and mood. Tell the studio which elements matter most and which can be interpreted. A reference should clarify the brief rather than leave the final composition ambiguous.

What to include in your enquiry

Send a wall photograph, approximate width and height, room palette, preferred subject or style, orientation and target date. Mention whether the artwork is for a home, office, reception or gift so the composition can be discussed in context.
